ANALYSIS:
The confusion matrix immediately reveals that this model classified all coins as quarters, all with relatively high confidence. The confidence scores tend to stay above 90%, but the outliers that display below 85% are telling of some of the real-world issues that the classifier is facing:
- dirty/worn coins
- unique reverse-side designs (especially quarters)
- slightly rotated coins
What may also explain the inaccuracy of this test are some key differences between the Kaggle dataset and my local dataset:
- zoom (how much image the coin takes up)
- background
- the model may have learned the wrong visual features
	- the training dataset (Kaggle) did have some slight differences between the backgrounds of the different denominations
		- quarters always have a completely white background
		- nickels have backgrounds that range from completely white to medium-gray and shadowy
		- pennies also have backgrounds that range from completely white to medium-gray and shadowy, but further:
			- pennies occasionally have markings in the background that would easily be picked up by edge detection

CONCLUSION:
	Coin rotation could potentially be addressed in the future using an edge detector to assert a specific orientation, than cropping the image accordingly. I would, however, worry about the near-infinite variations of designs, and thus edges, making it very difficult to assert an orientation. Another potential issue with rotating an image is the potential of lost data. 
	Dirty and worn coins could potentially be addressed by associating a wear rating with each coin, and factoring that into the denomination categorization calculation. However, only so much can be done when it comes to design recognition, worn lines/edges, and faded text. Even the data generation for training a wear rating model would be likely manual, making this a difficult task. 
	Unique coin designs could likely be addressed by favoring the obverse side of a given coin. The obverse side is less prone to variation than the reverse side, making it a better, more consistent base for training the model as well as testing it. 
	Zoom variation between the two datasets should be relatively easy to fix, in that I simply need to add to my image pre-processing script a section that will zoom in more. 
	Background variation between the two datasets is fixable, but it would require making an entirely new local dataset with a different background, which inevitably defeats the purpose of CoinVision's user friendly goal.
